# What we don't know, and limitations

> Paths such as `data/...` and `rules/...` are relative to the repository root. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## What we don't know

* **Cross-backend (replicated for the register rule only).** Everything was measured on `ibm_marrakesh`. Three Heron r2 devices exist on the account with different backend software versions (marrakesh 1.0.21, fez 1.3.37, kingston 1.0.0). The register-probe core (R10x2, R11x2, R12x2, split6, non-foldable overlap) was replicated on `ibm_kingston` (plan `234a8fad3bff`, 2026-11-16) and on `ibm_fez` (plan `faf87bdc3fa6`, submitted 2026-11-15 while the device was in maintenance, executed 2026-11-16) with **identical outcomes** on all three backends ([details](FINDINGS.md#cross-backend-replication-on-ibm_kingston-and-ibm_fez)). That is all: the **latency model, the stack-need shapes, the larger register probes, the historical 2026-11-06 probes and the fix-pack were not run on `ibm_kingston` or `ibm_fez`**, so for any other rule, or for a verdict on another device, the data are still `ibm_marrakesh` only. Five binary outcomes per device cannot rule out small cross-device differences.
* **What is and is not recorded about the backends.** Recorded (`data/run_metadata.json`): processor family/revision (Heron r2) and `backend_version` for the three devices read after the runs (marrakesh 1.0.21, fez 1.3.37 in maintenance at that time, kingston 1.0.0), and the same fields read just before submission for the round-4 plans (p1, v1, e1, e2, p2, p3). Not recorded: controller firmware, the calibration snapshot in force when each job ran, the `backend_version` at job time for rounds 1-3 and the fix-pack runs, and any server-side compiler version. The device versions differ, so a cross-backend difference could be a version difference.
* **Firmware and date.** Four dates (2026-11-06, 2026-11-09, 2026-11-15 on `ibm_marrakesh`; 2026-11-16 the five-probe `ibm_kingston` and `ibm_fez` replications (the latter submitted 2026-11-15); see the status line), controller firmware not exposed by the API. Limits, error codes and latencies can change with a firmware or compiler update. The four 2026-11-06 failures reproduced nine days later, which is the only evidence about stability over time; the 25 accepted 2026-11-06 probes (K64..K1024, W32..W256, F1_regs150, ...) were measured once, never repeated, and serve as the only PASS witnesses for some regions of the envelope; nothing but the five register-rule probes was re-run after 2026-11-15.
* **Latency.** The fixed part of the latency (293 +/- 45 ns in one fit, 180 +/- 91 ns with an input-count term) is not established; Bool vs Uint op classes and input count are collinear in the design; the null job reads +65 +/- 27 ns (a false increment of that size); the reference-placement check is unresolved (1.5 sigma); the Z/X-probe asymmetry appeared in one session and not in the other; depth independence is shown only for XOR (re-associable); multiply cost is bounded only weakly; per-op costs of compare and register-wide ops were never measured. The 96 ns "flat feedforward" and IBM's ~600 ns are different quantities.
* **What triggers 6066.** Both fix-pack v1 circuits that used a narrowing cast of a shifted wide register (shift by literal, then Uint(32) -> Uint(8)) were rejected with 6066, while fix-pack v2 (a different packing construction, same 32-bit register widths) was accepted 3 of 3. So register width alone is not the trigger, but whether the shift, the narrowing cast or the combination triggers it has **not been isolated**; the checker treats the observed construction as a hazard, not as a rule.
* **What triggers 6057.** No closed-form model survived out-of-sample (Section 2). The empirical envelope's PASS and FAIL predictions held in 6 of 6 frozen predictions, but 31 of the 65 unique accepted vectors are UNCERTAIN under it and nothing explains *why* the controller rejects what it rejects (register allocation is a hypothesis suggested by IBM's wording, not a finding).
* **Monotonicity is an assumption.** The envelope predicts PASS for a circuit that uses no more of every measured resource than an accepted one; nothing guarantees that a circuit with fewer registers, ops or stack depth cannot fail (for example through a resource the vector does not contain). The 29 rejected probes cannot prove it.
* **The readback is a sampling check, not exhaustive.** An accepted probe was run with prepared basis-state inputs (2 to 8 patterns, 200 shots each, informative patterns marked *); that shows the output responds to the operands the patterns touch, with readout noise limiting correctness to 0.68-1.00. It is not an exhaustive truth-table test on the QPU, round-1 acceptance probes used random inputs and a weak statistic, and a controller bug that only shows on other inputs would not be seen.
* **Same-backend repeat.** All five register-rule probes (R10x2, R11x2, R12x2, split6, non-foldable overlap) reproduced their earlier outcomes on `ibm_marrakesh` about 7.5 to 8 hours later (23:23 UTC, same UTC date; `properties_last_update` unchanged, so no recalibration is evidenced; [details](FINDINGS.md#replication-on-ibm_marrakesh-about-8-h-later)). The cross-device replications on `ibm_kingston` and `ibm_fez` are the entry at the top of this list.
* **Statistics of repeats.** Most probes were run once; the p1 round was executed twice (one accidental duplicate), R11x2 seven times in total, R10x8 at least four and R12x2 at least five times each.

## Limitations

* Measured on `ibm_marrakesh`, four dates: 2026-11-06 (29 envelope probes, historical, run once), 2026-11-09 (legacy latency ladder), 2026-11-15 (everything else), 2026-11-16 (the 5 register-rule probes replicated on `ibm_kingston` and `ibm_fez`); unknown controller firmware. Limits and latencies can change with firmware or backend; results older than a few weeks should be re-run.
* Latency: 5 shapes in this round plus a 4-job legacy ladder; one job per shape; headline errors are jackknife over 24 probes with probe chi2/dof up to 58; estimator choice moves point estimates by up to 440 ns;
  X-type and Z-type probes disagree; one replication within the day (the second session); the null job (a case identical to the reference) reads +65 +/- 27 ns, so a false increment of about that size exists.
* Op classes (Bool vs Uint), input count and fixed cost are collinear in the design; compare and register-wide (SIMD) ops have designed probes (`ideal` tier) but no data.
* The 6057 pool description is fitted after the fact on 25 probes (each executed twice in round 4); it has not been tested on held-out probes, ties at pool 14 and two exceptions exist, the second round-4 execution was an accidental duplicate (usable as a replicate, not an independent session), and the accounting of temporaries is a guess at the controller compiler's behaviour.
* "Pre-registered" means written into the manifest before submission, with no external timestamp; later rounds were designed after earlier results.
